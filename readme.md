GreenThreads Operations Analysis
About This Project

This portfolio brings together my work from the GreenThreads operations project. The project focused on analyzing the operational risks involved in opening a new Denver store, including supplier reliability, shipment timing, product availability, workflow issues, and financial exposure.

Throughout the project, I used AI as an analytical tool to help organize information, review documents, analyze shipment data, and develop a custom assistant. I also tested the limitations of AI and verified important findings against the original project information. The goal was not simply to use AI, but to use it responsibly while applying human judgment to the results.

The Business Problem

GreenThreads needed to determine whether the Denver store could open on schedule while managing supplier and shipment risks. The analysis required information from multiple sources, including operational workflows, project documents, shipment data, and supplier agreements.

A major challenge was that findings from different functions did not always agree. In Week 9, the project revealed a contradiction between functional analyses. Rather than averaging the findings, I treated the disagreement as an important finding that needed to be investigated and reconciled.

Project Deliverables
HW#1 — Workflow Analysis

Analysis of the GreenThreads operations workflow, including process issues, opportunities for improvement, and areas where AI could support the work.

HW#2 — Document Analysis

Review of GreenThreads project documents to identify important requirements, deadlines, supplier information, and contractual details relevant to the Denver opening.

HW#3 — Data Analysis

Analysis of shipment data to evaluate supplier delivery performance and identify potential risks to the Denver store opening.

HW#4 — Custom AI Assistant

Development and testing of a custom assistant designed to support GreenThreads operations analysis. The assistant was tested to identify situations where its answers could be incorrect or require human verification.

Key Findings

The project identified shipment and supplier reliability issues that could affect the Denver store opening. Comparing promised delivery dates with actual delivery performance provided evidence for evaluating supplier reliability. The project also showed that important contract terms and delivery requirements must be verified against the original documents before management takes action.

Another important finding was the disagreement between functional analyses in Week 9. Each function's conclusion was supported by its own available data, but the conclusions did not completely align. This demonstrated the importance of investigating the source of disagreements rather than assuming that one analysis or the other is automatically correct.

How I Used AI

AI helped speed up several parts of the analytical process. I used it to organize information, review documents, analyze data, identify potential patterns, and develop a repeatable analytical workflow.

However, I did not treat AI-generated information as automatically correct. Testing the custom assistant revealed situations where its responses could be incomplete or incorrect. Important dates, contract terms, calculations, and conclusions therefore required verification against the original project materials.

This project taught me that the analyst's role is not replaced by AI. AI can help make the work faster, but the analyst provides context, judgment, verification, and business meaning.
